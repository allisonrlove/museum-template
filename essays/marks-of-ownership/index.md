Long before signatures, people needed a way to say that something was theirs, or that a message came from someone with authority. They solved the problem with small objects that could be pressed, carried or worn, and the results are some of the most intimate evidence we have of how power worked.

## Pressing a name into clay

The [Mitanni Cylinder Seal]({{ site.baseurl }}/objects/mitanni-cylinder-seal) is a signature in the form of a stone. Rolled across wet clay, it left a scene that proved a jar had not been opened or a document had been approved.

## Announcing a marriage

The [Scarab of Queen Tiye]({{ site.baseurl }}/objects/scarab-of-queen-tiye) works differently. It was not a private mark but a public message, produced in numbers so that news of a royal marriage could travel.

{% include essay/object.html slug="scarab-of-queen-tiye" note="The same text on many scarabs: an announcement made portable." %}

## Wearing status

By the time the [Tsuba]({{ site.baseurl }}/objects/tsuba-sword-guard) was made, the sword at its centre was rarely drawn. The guard had become the place where its owner's taste was displayed.
