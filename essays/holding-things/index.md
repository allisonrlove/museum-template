A container is a decision. Someone chose what to keep, how to protect it and how much to spend doing it. Looked at together, a bronze offering vessel, a ceramic tomb vessel and two glass containers show how the same basic idea can serve ritual, trade and everyday life.

## Containers for the dead and for the gods

The [Ritual Grain Vessel]({{ site.baseurl }}/objects/zhou-ritual-vessel) and the [Maya vessel]({{ site.baseurl }}/objects/maya-double-vessel) were both made for occasions rather than for the kitchen. Their value lay in the ceremony, and the object survives where the contents did not.

## Glass changes the rules

By the time of the [Roman flask]({{ site.baseurl }}/objects/roman-glass-flask), blowing made containers fast to produce. The [Cosmetic Jar]({{ site.baseurl }}/objects/cosmetic-jar) shows the opposite instinct, glass used to signal that what lay inside was precious.
