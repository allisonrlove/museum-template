The sky was the first public clock, calendar and map. Everyone could see it, but not everyone read it the same way. These three objects show that measuring the heavens and respecting them were often the same act.

## A model of the heavens

The [Planispheric Astrolabe]({{ site.baseurl }}/objects/planispheric-astrolabe) is a calculator in brass. Its plates, engraved for particular latitudes, turn the position of a star into the time of day.

{% include essay/object.html slug="planispheric-astrolabe" note="A handheld instrument that was also a work of fine craft." %}

## A defence against eclipses

The dragons on the [Bowl with Dragons]({{ site.baseurl }}/objects/bowl-with-dragons) have been read as protection against eclipses, which suggests the sky was something to guard against as well as to chart.

## Below the sky

For the Maya, the heavens were part of how the world was organised. The [double-chambered vessel]({{ site.baseurl }}/objects/maya-double-vessel) belongs to a culture that tracked the cycles of the sun, moon and Venus with great accuracy and made rituals around them.
